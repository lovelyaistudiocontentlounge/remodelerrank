# Ad Creative Skill

Read this file completely before writing any ad copy, landing page copy, email subject lines, or social content for RemodelerRank or for any contractor client.

## What this skill does

Guides copy for contractor-facing ads, landing pages, emails, Instagram captions, and any other channel where we need to speak directly to a remodeling contractor. The goal is always the same: name the pain, name the cost of the pain, and make the next step obvious.

---

## Copy Formulas

### Formula 1 - Pain + Cost + Fix
> "[Pain statement]. [What it costs them]. [What we do about it]."

Example: "Your competitor shows up in Google Maps and you don't. The contractor in the top 3 gets 10x the clicks. We fix that."

### Formula 2 - Observation + Question
> "[Specific true observation about their situation]. [Question that makes them feel seen]."

Example: "Homeowners searched for a remodeler in Walnut Creek last night. Which contractor called you?"

### Formula 3 - Stakes First
> "[The dollar amount or loss]. [Why that number is real]. [What prevents it]."

Example: "One missed kitchen remodel is $85K+. That's more than a year of marketing. You can't afford an invisible website."

## Contractor Pain Language — Why It Matters to Them

Every piece of copy we write speaks to one or more of these five pains.
Always name the pain AND the cost of the pain. Never just describe a feature.

### The 5 Contractor Pains

**1. Invisible on Google**
Pain: "My competitor shows up and I don't."
What it costs them: The contractor in the top 3 gets 10x the clicks.
Every day they don't rank is a day homeowners call someone else.
Copy trigger: "Homeowners searched for a remodeler in [city] last night.
Which contractor called you?"

**2. Website that doesn't convert**
Pain: "I have a website but it doesn't do anything."
What it costs them: Homeowners decide in 8 seconds. A bad site doesn't
just look unprofessional — it actively sends them to a competitor.
Copy trigger: "Homeowners make a decision before they scroll."

**3. Referral dependency**
Pain: "I'm living referral to referral — I can't control it."
What it costs them: Feast and famine. No pipeline means laying off crew
when referrals dry up. One slow quarter can undo years of work.
Copy trigger: "Referrals are great. You cannot control them."

**4. Invisible GBP**
Pain: "My Google profile is half-finished and I don't know what to do with it."
What it costs them: GBP is the first thing homeowners see.
A half-finished profile loses jobs every single day — silently.
Copy trigger: "A half-finished GBP loses you jobs every day. Silently."

**5. Wrong jobs / tire kickers**
Pain: "I'm getting leads but they're the wrong ones — small budgets,
unrealistic expectations."
What it costs them: Wasted estimates, wasted time, wasted crew days.
The real cost is the $85K+ job they didn't quote because they were tied up
on a $15K job that came in through a bad website.
Copy trigger: "Sick of $30K budgets for $160K projects?"

### The Stakes Line
Always be able to answer: "What does it cost them if this problem doesn't get fixed?"
The answer for NorCal remodelers: One missed kitchen remodel = $85K+.
That is more than a year of marketing at any tier.
Use this math when the copy needs weight.

### Voice Rules
- Speak to the contractor as a peer, not a vendor
- Name their specific city when possible
- Use their words: "tire kickers", "living referral to referral",
  "feast and famine", "the estimate that didn't close"
- No hype. No exclamation points. No "amazing" or "incredible"
- Honest grades, honest observations, honest copy — always
