## Existing Identity

Logo characteristics: sourced 2026-09-11 from sw-cabinets.com
(`logo/sw-logo-large.png`). An ornate, illustrated mark: a cabinet armoire
door with an S/W monogram set into each door panel, decorative scrollwork
flourishes flanking both sides, "Custom Cabinets Inc." in a serif wordmark
below a rule line. Traditional/heritage character, hand-drawn feeling, not
minimal or modern.

**Real tension worth naming:** this logo's register (ornate, traditional,
illustrated) sits at odds with the modern-minimal Inter-based direction this
mockup is built in (itself derived from Medallion's restrained, contemporary
system). The mockup currently treats the logo as a small trust anchor in the
nav and lets the modern system dominate everywhere else - workable, but not
a resolved decision. The alternative is letting the real mark pull the whole
direction warmer/more traditional (a different typographic and color
system entirely). Worth a real call once this moves past rough draft, not
left as a default.

Existing colors: not assessed with real hex extraction - site reads neutral/
contemporary in this pass

Photography style: strong - warm, natural-toned, real project photography,
well-organized by category on Instagram (vanities, kitchens, islands, custom
doors, home offices, wine/coffee bars, furniture hoods). This is a genuine
asset, not a gap - unusual for a prospect this pipeline sees.

Product/design style: custom kitchens, vanities, closets, home offices,
entertainment/media built-ins - broad residential cabinetry range

## Voice

Representative phrases from existing brand:
1. "Family owned and operated" (Instagram bio, primary site)
2. "Operating for over 20 years, SW is family-owned and services all of the
   Bay Area and Northern California" (sw-cabinets.com/about)
3. "Servicing the construction industry for 25 years" (Instagram bio)
4. "Create your dream home" (sw-cabinets.com)
5. "Premium Bay Area Kitchens" (702kitchens.com tagline - notably more
   upscale-positioned language than the plain, family-owned tone of the main
   site - this inconsistency itself is a finding, see audit)

Common vocabulary: family owned, custom, dream home, years of experience/
craftsmanship

Tone: plain and service-focused on sw-cabinets.com; noticeably more sales/
lifestyle-aspirational on 702kitchens.com ("luxury estates"). This split voice
across two domains for one business is itself a problem worth naming, not just
a style note - a prospect who lands on one domain gets a different personality
than one who lands on the other.

Technical vs conversational: conversational, not technical - no engineering/
precision language like AF Woodworker uses

Traditional vs contemporary: contemporary, per the photography and copy

Reserved vs expressive: reserved/plain on the main site

Words/phrases to avoid: heavy sales/lifestyle language like 702kitchens.com's
"luxury estates" - if the real work is genuinely high-end, it should prove that
through actual project photography and specifics (scope, materials, the
designer/architect relationships if confirmed), not adjective-stacking. The
gap here isn't earning trust from zero, it's making real, substantial work
visible instead of asserting quality in marketing language.

## Visual References

### Reference 1 - Jennifer's pick: medallioncabinetry.com
What it is: Medallion Cabinetry's own site - a national cabinet
*manufacturer* selling semi-custom product lines (Silver/Gold/Platinum/
Frameless tiers) through dealer showrooms. Restrained neutral palette (white/
black/gray), generous whitespace, clean modern sans-serif type, photography-led
hero with real finish/wood-grain detail shown without heavy filters, and a
customer-journey nav (Why Medallion / New Arrivals / Products / Plan /
Inspiration) instead of a generic Services/About/Contact structure. Full
technical breakdown of the site's actual scroll mechanism (a pinned,
scroll-scrubbed GSAP sequence, not simple parallax) in
`design-territories/medallion-reference-analysis.md` - this is genuinely
portable to a static site build, not a WordPress-only trick.
What's useful: The restraint itself - letting real photography and material
detail carry the premium feeling instead of sales copy or ornamentation. The
journey-based navigation is also directly portable (SW's real audience needs
"see the work / understand the process / start a project," not a product
catalog).
What NOT to copy: The product-tier shopping structure (Silver/Gold/Platinum/
Frameless) and dealer/showroom-locator model - these exist because Medallion
is a manufacturer selling standardized lines through third parties. SW does
one-off bespoke work directly for named clients, so a "shop by tier" structure
would misrepresent the actual business and lose the real differentiator
(every piece here is custom, not selected off a line card).
How it translates: Keep the neutral, restrained palette and generous
whitespace; keep photography (SW's own real project photos, which are
already strong per the audit) as the dominant visual content rather than
copy; keep a customer-journey nav instead of a generic one. Replace the
product-tier browsing with real, named project case studies - this is the one
canonical site that should absorb whatever's worth keeping from both
sw-cabinets.com and 702kitchens.com.

### Reference 2

### Reference 3
What it is: A single-location family business site that still reads
substantial and current (avoiding the "family owned" framing feeling small or
dated next to real high-end work)
What's useful: Shows that "family owned, 20+ years" and "does substantial
high-end work" are not in tension - the two-site split makes it feel like they
are
What NOT to copy: Overcorrecting into corporate/agency tone, which would lose
the real family-business trust signal
How it translates: One site that carries both facts at once instead of
splitting them across a plain domain and a "luxury" domain
