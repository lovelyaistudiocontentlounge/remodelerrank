Social Content

A format built to actually get found

Your own Instagram captions already have a real voice, plain, factual, project-and-location-named. The one thing missing is a real hook before the facts, right now the captions open with the subject instead of earning the stop. Pair that fix with the tagging that actually drives reach and local search, not just a caption that reads well.

Hook, Then Context
A real hook first (see social-captions/SKILL.md), the specific project details second, never the reverse.
"A wine wall this size usually means a dozen panels fighting each other. This one reads as a single piece of walnut.
Book-matched walnut, custom wine wall at Resolute, San Francisco, built entirely in-house from planning through install."
"Most bar counters look good on opening night. Fewer look good after a decade of real service.
Champion's Bar at the Olympic Club, San Francisco, walnut counter and back-bar millwork built to the same standard Arnold + Egan has run on hospitality work since 1937."

What to tag, every time

The Partners

Tag the architect, designer, and GC on every post where they're known. Each one has their own followers, most of whom are exactly the kind of client who hires a millwork shop, and a tagged post shows up on their profile too, real reach at zero cost.

The Venue

Tag the client's own business account (Blue Bottle, Twitch, the restaurant or bar), if they post it or comment on it, that's visibility to their audience directly, not just yours.

The Location

Use Instagram's actual location pin, not just the city name in the caption, dropped on the real project address or neighborhood. For any area where more work is actively wanted, this is the single most direct lever, Instagram and Google both use it to connect a business to that specific place.

Why this matters more than the caption itself: a great caption with no tags reaches your own followers. The same caption with the architect, the venue, and a real location pin reaches three networks and shows up in local discovery, for the same five minutes of work.

Proof, For the Right Audience

Zero reviews matters less here than it looks

Zero reviews on Google or Yelp is a real, visible gap, but weigh it against who's actually deciding: GCs, architects, and designers who already work off referral and direct reference, not homeowners comparing star ratings before they call. The stronger move for your audience is a direct reference or a LinkedIn recommendation from a GC or architect who's actually worked with you, not a public review campaign built for a residential business.

Draft Email, Personalize Before Sending, GC or Architect Contact
Subject: A quick ask, from Arnold + Egan Hi [Name], It's been a while since we wrapped [project name] together, and we're still glad we got to work with you on it. We're doing a better job of showing our real work and real relationships going forward. Would you be open to a short LinkedIn recommendation, or just giving us the OK to list you as a reference the next time a GC or architect asks who we've worked with? Either one helps, no pressure on which. Thank you for the work together. We'd take another project with you any time. Kenneth Egan Arnold + Egan
Why this works: it targets the people who actually decide, not a mass review push. A LinkedIn recommendation or a named reference carries real weight with another GC or architect in a way a five-star Google rating doesn't.
