## Existing Identity

Logo characteristics: **A real logo exists**, found embedded in
`photos/raw/ae-residential-kitchen-collage-with-logo.jpg` - a geometric
mark: a square containing an inscribed circle and crossing diagonal lines,
forming an abstract compass/wheel shape, paired with "ARNOLD + EGAN" set in
wide-tracked caps below. Confident, architectural, precise - fits a company
whose actual craft is precision millwork. The live site's own modern
sans-serif typography and minimal black/white palette confirm this reading
directly (2026-09-15) - the mark's geometric precision isn't an isolated
flourish, it matches the whole identity. **Corrected 2026-09-15**: the
real logo lives on their own site (not extracted from a photo) -
`logo/ae-logo-real-horizontal.png` and `ae-logo-real-icon-wordmark.png`,
official assets, replacing an earlier hand-cropped version. Jennifer's
own read on the mark: the crossing diagonals inside the circle read as an
abstracted "A + E" monogram, not just a generic geometric compass shape -
a modern identity choice, notably not a traditional/heritage mark despite
the real 89-year history behind it.

Existing colors: Confirmed from the live site (2026-09-15) - black as the
primary logo/text color, white/cream background, a deliberately minimal
palette. No color accent in their current digital identity at all, which
leaves real room for the design territory to introduce one (the walnut
lattice piece's warm wood tone is the obvious, earned candidate, not an
invented one).

Photography style: Strong, professional, varied - real commercial
photography (not phone snapshots) across genuinely different venue types.

Primary materials: Wood (walnut tones visible in the lattice piece and the
ROAM bar counter), painted white millwork (residential kitchen work), mixed
materials depending on commercial project.

Product/design style: Ranges widely by project - traditional white
residential kitchens, warm mid-century-modern lattice work, rustic-industrial
(ROAM burger counter), classic private-club paneling (Champion's Bar),
Mediterranean-restaurant warmth (arched windows, olive trees). This range
itself may be the real story, not any single style.

## Voice

Confirmed 2026-09-15 from arnoldandegan.com/about, consistent with the one
Instagram caption on file:

Representative phrases from existing brand:
1. "Full-Service Casework & Millwork Manufacturing Company"
2. "Commercial, Hospitality, Retail and Residential Clients"
3. Take "any project from the Architectural & Planning stages and see it
   through to completion"
4. "High-Quality Fixtures that are personalized to their unique
   specifications"
5. "Custom Millwork at Champion's Bar, Olympic Club San Francisco"
   (Instagram caption style - plain, project-and-location-named)

**Mission statement (found 2026-09-15):** "To Build Projects Inspired by
Architects & Designers that exceed the Needs of our Clients." Real,
load-bearing fact - positions them explicitly as an execution partner FOR
the architect/design community first, not a direct-to-homeowner shop that
happens to also do commercial work. This should shape the whole site's
framing more than any other single line found so far.

Common vocabulary: full-service, personalized, specifications, planning
through completion - capability and process language, not lifestyle or
sales language.

Tone: professional, confident, understated - competence and scope carry
the weight, not adjective-stacking. No tagline, no mission statement; the
89 years does the persuading implicitly rather than being asserted.

Technical vs conversational: closer to technical than AF or SW - "casework,"
"fixtures," "specifications" read like real trade vocabulary, not
consumer-facing softened language.

Traditional vs contemporary: the copy voice is timeless/plain, but the
visual execution (typography, palette) is contemporary, not a heritage
pastiche - matches the logo mark's modern-geometric register, not the
89-year history's "should feel old" assumption.

Reserved vs expressive: reserved - the site does not oversell scope or
history, it states it.

Words/phrases to avoid: any invented mission statement or tagline (they
don't have one and the reserved tone doesn't call for adding one),
lifestyle/luxury language (misfits the trade-vocabulary register), and
overplaying the 89-year history as nostalgia rather than as a working
capability track record.

## Physical Analogy

If Arnold + Egan's brand were a physical object rather than a website, what
would it be?

Answer: **A manufacturer's capabilities book from a firm that's been doing
this since 1937** - not a trendy lookbook, a real institutional record of
range and precision, the kind of object a GC or hospitality designer would
actually keep on a shelf and reference.

Why: The real asset here isn't a single aesthetic, it's depth (89 years) and
breadth (hospitality to retail to residential) - a capabilities book format
lets both show without forcing a single stylistic identity onto work that
is deliberately varied by client.

## Visual References

### Reference 1 - the lattice screen itself (Jennifer's flagged signature element)

What it is: `photos/raw/ae-lattice-wood-screen-detail.jpg` - an extreme
macro shot of a diamond-pattern walnut lattice panel, precisely cut,
mid-century-modern in feeling.

What's useful: This is real Arnold + Egan work, not a borrowed reference -
it's usable directly as a signature visual motif (a repeating diamond-lattice
graphic device for section dividers, a background texture, a navigational
element, or a literal photographic hero) BECAUSE it's their own craft, not
an aesthetic borrowed from someone else's site.

What NOT to do: Don't let the lattice motif become decorative wallpaper -
it should read as evidence of precision (the way AF Woodworker's spec-card
device shows technical process), not just a pretty repeating pattern.

How it translates: A genuine candidate for the site's signature visual
device - unlike AF Woodworker (no logo) or SW (logo doesn't match the
chosen modern-minimal direction), Arnold + Egan has BOTH a real logo whose
geometric language (circles, squares, crossing lines) and the lattice's
diamond-grid geometry actually rhyme with each other. That's a rare, real
alignment worth building the whole direction around rather than treating as
a separate flourish.
