What "Full-Service" Actually Means When You're Hiring a Millwork Shop

Short answer Full-service means the shop is involved from the planning and specification stage through design, fabrication, and installation, one point of responsibility the whole way, instead of being handed a finished drawing to execute after the real decisions are already made elsewhere.

Most millwork shops start work once a design is finalized: a designer or architect hands over drawings, and the shop builds to spec. That's fabrication, and it's a real, useful service. It's not the same thing as full-service, even when a shop's marketing uses the word.

What changes when a shop is involved from planning

Design and specification decisions get made with fabrication constraints already in mind, not discovered after the drawings are locked. A detail that looks fine on paper but is genuinely difficult or expensive to actually build gets caught and adjusted before it becomes a change order mid-project.

It also means one point of contact for the entire arc of the work: the same team that helped plan it is the team that builds it and the team that installs it. Nothing gets lost in a handoff between a design phase, a fabrication phase, and an installation phase run by three different companies who've never talked to each other directly.

How to tell the difference when you're hiring

Ask directly: does the shop want to see the project before the design is finalized, or only after? A shop that only wants finished drawings is a fabricator, a fine thing to hire, just not what "full-service" is supposed to mean.
