## Territory - "The Capabilities Record"

Concept name: The Capabilities Record (directly from Brand DNA's Physical
Analogy: "a manufacturer's capabilities book from a firm that's been doing
this since 1937" - not a new metaphor, the one already earned).

Core idea: The real asset here isn't one aesthetic, it's depth (89 years)
and breadth (hospitality, retail, food service, wellness, residential) that
their own site currently doesn't organize or show. So the site itself is
structured like a capabilities record: a real institutional reference
document a GC or hospitality designer would keep on a shelf, organized by
what they can build across sectors, not a single stylistic mood board. This
is the one deliberate structural departure from both SW and AF - those
sites are organized as a single continuous narrative (one family, one shop
scrolling top to bottom); this one is organized as categories of proof,
because the range across sectors is the actual differentiator.

Why it fits: Confirmed directly against the real site (2026-09-15) - "Full-
Service Casework & Millwork Manufacturing Company," "Commercial,
Hospitality, Retail and Residential Clients," "personalized to their unique
specifications." The voice is reserved and capability-focused, never
promotional; the structure should carry that same reserve rather than
oversell with adjective-stacked copy.

Typographic direction: A confident modern sans matching the real logo's
wide-tracked-caps wordmark for headings (uppercase, generous letter-
spacing, restrained) - Neue Montreal or similar geometric grotesk. A plain
workhorse sans for body copy (their "casework," "fixtures,"
"specifications" trade vocabulary reads better in something quietly
technical than something warm or humanist). No serif anywhere - the
89-year history does not need a heritage typographic costume; the real
site already proved contemporary execution fits this company better than
a nostalgia treatment would.

Color behavior: Base stays their real identity - black on white/cream,
confirmed from the live site, genuinely minimal. Earn exactly ONE accent
rather than inventing one: the warm walnut/bronze tone that already
appears in both signature photos (the lattice screen's walnut, the bronze
filigree hardware against green marble). Used sparingly - a rule, a label,
a hover state - never as a background wash, keeping the reserved black/
white register intact.

Layout / composition: The work section organizes by SECTOR (Hospitality /
Retail & Food Service / Wellness / Residential), not a flat photo grid -
the structural signature of this territory and the thing that makes the
range itself legible instead of just implied. Each sector gets a named
section with its own real project(s), not a mixed grid where the range has
to be inferred by the viewer. This is a genuinely different information
architecture from SW's single bento-grid gallery and AF's single 12-col
spec-plate grid, not just a different visual skin on the same idea - the
anti-cloning lesson from AF applied at the structural level, not just CSS.

Signature visual device: The diamond-grid motif, drawn directly from the
real lattice screen photo's geometry AND the real logo's crossing-diagonal
geometry - a genuine, earned rhyme between two pieces of real material, not
an invented pattern (unlike AF, where the diamond motif stands in for a
logo that doesn't exist yet). Used as section-divider marks between sector
categories and as a small corner accent on project entries - evidence of
precision, the way the brief for AF's spec-plate device already warned
against letting a motif become decorative wallpaper. Never a full
background texture.

Hero: The bronze filigree hardware shot (`ae-bronze-filigree-marble-
counter-shop.jpg`), captured mid-fabrication in their own shop - real
craft-in-progress, not a finished room. Stronger hero material than
anything in the original 8-photo set: it does more work in one frame than
a finished kitchen could (visible tools, workbench, materials mid-assembly
= literal evidence of "personalized to their unique specifications").

What NOT to do: Don't let the 89-year history become the headline framing
("since 1937" as the hero line) - the real site itself doesn't lead with
history, it leads with capability and scope. History supports the case
(a "Founded 1937" detail, a footer line), it shouldn't be the opening
argument. Don't invent a tagline or mission statement - confirmed they
don't have one and the reserved tone doesn't call for adding one.

## Structural additions (2026-09-15, post-feedback)

Jennifer's real critique of the first pass: it read as a design refresh, not
a reason to switch. A prospect's own current site does not need to be ugly
for this to fail as a pitch, it needs to be thin - no Services, no Process,
no content engine. Three sections added directly for this:

- **Services**, named and specific (Custom Casework & Cabinetry,
  Architectural Millwork, Commercial & Hospitality Fixtures, Design &
  Specification, In-House Fabrication, Installation & Completion) - not
  invented, each drawn from the real about-page scope of work and what the
  photos already show.
- **Process**, a four-step planning-through-completion timeline - this is
  their own "take any project from the Architectural & Planning stages...
  through to completion" line, expanded into something a prospect can
  actually see, not just read.
- **Guides/AEO section** - the same content-freshness device already
  proven on SW's mockup (placeholder titles, a note that real articles run
  through RemodelerRank's own AEO pipeline), restyled in A+E's palette.
  This is the argument for why a prospect should keep coming back, not just
  visit once - a real site should show up in search over time, not be a
  one-time brochure.
