## Territory A - Product

Derived primarily from what the company physically makes.

Concept name: The Working Record

Core idea: Let the craft object carry the whole page. Hero and photography
throughout are extreme material detail, not finished rooms - the bronze
filigree hardware shot mid-fabrication in their own shop, the walnut
lattice screen, joinery close-ups. The site reads like flipping through a
maker's own reference book, not a sales brochure. Copy stays minimal and
technical; the objects do the persuading.

Why it fits: This is the one client in the portfolio with real
in-progress-craft photography (the bronze filigree shot, unlike anything
SW or AF has), and a logo whose geometry (square, circle, crossing
diagonals) already rhymes with the real lattice screen's diamond grid - a
genuine, earned visual system to build the whole direction around.

Typographic direction: A technical, drafting-adjacent register - a
monospace or engineering-style numeral treatment for labels and specs,
paired with the confident sans already confirmed from their real logo
(Archivo or similar). Numbers and measurements get real visual weight.

Color behavior: Their real black/white minimalism, unchanged. The only
accent is the warm bronze/walnut tone already present in the real
photography - never a color chosen for the page, always one found in the
work itself.

Layout / composition: Organized by MATERIAL/TECHNIQUE rather than sector
- Woodwork, Metalwork & Hardware, Stone & Surface - so the page teaches
how a fixture actually gets made, not just what venue it ended up in.

Photography treatment: Macro-forward everywhere, including a full section
that is nothing but material/joinery details with no context shot at all
- deliberately unusual for a contractor site, which is what makes it feel
like real expertise instead of a portfolio.

Navigation approach: A short, technical nav (Materials / Capabilities /
Contact) - fewer stops than a typical marketing site, matching the
"reference book" feeling.

Signature visual device: The diamond motif (drawn from the real logo's
crossing diagonals) used as a literal drafting/measurement grid overlay on
hero and section-divider moments - not decoration, evidence of precision.

Motion / interaction: A slow scroll-driven zoom that starts tight on a
material detail (the bronze filigree) and pulls back only enough to reveal
context, never a full room. Restrained, matches the "reference book" tone
rather than a cinematic reveal.

Risks: Could read cold or inaccessible to a residential homeowner
comparing contractors casually - this territory is built for someone who
already values precision (a GC, an architect), not for someone who needs
to be sold on why craft matters in the first place.

What makes this different from the other portfolio sites: SW and AF are
both organized around finished rooms and photogenic results. This is the
only territory in the whole portfolio organized around the making
process itself, not the outcome.

## Territory B - Customer

Derived primarily from how its ideal customer evaluates and buys.

Concept name: The Capabilities Record

Core idea: A GC, architect, or hospitality developer vetting a millwork
manufacturer needs three things fast: proof of real clients, proof of
range across project types, and a clear sense of how the process actually
works. This territory puts all three front and center, organized the way
a buyer's own checklist works, not the way a homeowner browses a
portfolio.

Why it fits: This is the client with the strongest name-recognition proof
in the whole portfolio - Twitch, Blue Bottle Coffee, Champion's Bar at the
Olympic Club are all real, checkable, impressive. A buyer-evaluation
territory is the one that gets the most mileage out of that fact,
something SW and AF's audits never surfaced.

Typographic direction: Confident, slightly corporate-adjacent but still
warm - a clean geometric sans for headings (Archivo, matching the real
logo's tracked-caps wordmark), a quietly technical body face (IBM Plex
Sans or similar) that reads like real trade vocabulary, not consumer
marketing copy.

Color behavior: Same real black/white base, one bronze/walnut accent
earned from the photography - kept identical across territories since
it's their real identity, not a territory-specific choice.

Layout / composition: Organized by SECTOR (Hospitality / Retail & Food
Service / Wellness / Residential), the way a buyer's own mental model of
"who else like me has hired them" works. Real named clients surface early
and often, not buried in captions.

Photography treatment: A mix of context (the finished space, so a buyer
can picture their own project) and one or two process shots to prove the
craft claim, not the exclusively macro treatment Territory A uses.

Navigation approach: A fuller, buyer-journey nav (Services / Capabilities
/ Process / The Work / Guides / Start a Project) - more stops than
Territory A, because a buyer comparing vendors wants to see the whole
case made, not just be impressed by one object.

Signature visual device: A "trusted by" strip naming real clients directly
(Twitch, Blue Bottle Coffee, Champion's Bar) near the top of the page,
before any sector detail - the fastest possible trust signal for someone
in evaluation mode.

Motion / interaction: Minimal, functional. This territory is not selling
itself on spectacle - a simple, restrained hero (no elaborate scroll
sequence) keeps the page feeling efficient and buyer-focused rather than
cinematic.

Risks: Without the craft photography carrying real visual weight, this
could tip into reading like a generic B2B services site - the sector
structure and named clients do real work, but they are not enough alone
to prove precision; the process/material photography still has to be
genuinely strong throughout.

What makes this different from the other portfolio sites: Neither SW nor
AF has real named commercial clients to lean on - this is the only
portfolio site where "who else has hired them" is a legitimate,
answerable, impressive question, and the whole territory is built to let
a buyer ask and answer it fast.

## Territory C - Story

Derived primarily from company history, personality, process, or
differentiator.

**Revised 2026-09-15** after Jennifer's direct input: the original draft
of this territory led with a literal decade-by-decade timeline, which she
correctly flagged as pulling the wrong direction. Real findings changed
the shape of this territory: a real mission statement ("To Build Projects
Inspired by Architects & Designers that exceed the Needs of our Clients"),
confirmed 1937 founding (via LinkedIn, resolving a conflicting "1977"
directory listing), zero public reviews anywhere (BuildZoom explicitly
confirms none, despite a 96 score), and the owner's real standing in
industry/community circles (per Jennifer, not yet independently sourced).
The company is genuinely modern - CNC fabrication, a geometric logo that
reads as an abstracted "A+E" monogram, not a heritage mark - despite 89
real years behind it. The story here is not nostalgia, it is standing.

Concept name: Built for the Design Community

Core idea: Lead with the real mission statement - they exist to execute
what architects and designers design, and to exceed the client's needs
doing it. Ninety years of institutional depth is the proof behind that
claim, not the headline. No reviews exist to lean on (a real, checked
fact), so the credibility case runs entirely through standing: the
license, the BuildZoom 96, the confirmed 1937 founding, and the owner's
real professional community presence. The page reads like it was built to
earn an architect's trust, not a homeowner's.

Why it fits: This is the only territory that uses the mission statement
at all - the single most load-bearing sentence found in this whole
research pass, and previously reduced to a stat-strip number in both
other territories. It also resolves Territory C's original risk directly:
a mission-and-standing story does not depend on period photography or
nostalgia the way a literal timeline would have, so the "if period
material is thin, this territory has nothing to fill itself with" problem
goes away.

Typographic direction: Modern, matching the real logo and the real site -
not a heritage/traditional register. Same confident sans as Territories
A/B (Archivo or similar for headings), used with more restraint and
whitespace than Territory B's fuller buyer-journey layout, closer to a
firm's own statement of purpose than a services menu.

Color behavior: Same real black/white base, one bronze/walnut accent
earned from the photography - unchanged across all three territories,
since it is their real identity, not a territory-specific choice.

Layout / composition: Opens on the mission statement itself, full-bleed,
minimal - a statement of purpose, not a hero pitch. Followed by a
standing section (license, BuildZoom score, 1937, the owner's professional
community presence, once Jennifer supplies specifics) presented as
credentials, not history for its own sake. The real work follows as
evidence the mission is actually being met, not the other way around.

Photography treatment: The strongest present-day craft shots carry the
page (the bronze filigree hardware mid-fabrication, the lattice screen,
the walnut joinery detail) - no attempt to source or fabricate period
material, since the story here is standing, not nostalgia.

Navigation approach: A short, purpose-led nav (Mission / Standing / The
Work / Start a Project) - fewer stops than Territory B, and none of them
"About" in the generic sense.

Signature visual device: The logo's A+E monogram, presented large and
literally at the mission-statement moment - a modern, geometric identity
device standing in for a "founding" moment, replacing the timeline crawl
from the original draft. The monogram already carries the "since 1937"
weight without needing a literal decade-by-decade device to earn it.

Motion / interaction: A single, restrained scroll-driven reveal on the
mission statement itself (text and monogram resolve into focus together
as the page opens) - not the elaborate multi-scene sequence originally
proposed. Answers the "pan out / scroll motion" direction with one
deliberate moment instead of an extended sequence, matching this
territory's more restrained, standing-not-spectacle register.

Risks: Depends on Jennifer supplying real specifics for the owner's
community/industry involvement - without them, "standing" is three real
facts (license, BuildZoom, 1937) and one vague gesture, which is thinner
than it should be. Also depends on keeping genuinely modern throughout -
any lapse into heritage/traditional visual language anywhere on the page
would contradict the whole premise.

What makes this different from the other portfolio sites: Neither SW nor
AF has a real mission statement this specific, nor a credibility case that
has to run entirely without reviews - this is the only portfolio site
built around institutional standing instead of either finished-room
photography (SW, AF) or named-client social proof (Territory B here).
