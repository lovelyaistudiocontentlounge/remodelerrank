## 4. Message Inventory

**Words they naturally use:** "custom," "modern," "sleek," "minimalist," "tailor-made,"
"craftsmanship," "precision," "full-scale installation," "design consultation"

**Words they'd probably never use:** anything overtly luxury/exclusive ("bespoke,"
"opulent") - their register is craft/engineering, not luxury-lifestyle. Also
unlikely to use heavy sales language ("limited time," "don't miss out") - the
existing copy is calm and descriptive, not pushy.

**Claims found:** "modern, sleek, and fully customized cabinetry," "high-quality
work with the professionalism and expertise clients can trust," CNC/props/acrylic
capability, 2D/3D visualization before production.

**Customer language (from their own testimonials):** "professionalism and
attention to detail," "fantastic job," "incredibly high quality with clearly
durable materials," "asked for my input each step of the way," "would 100% hire
them again." Recurring customer theme: collaborative design process + durable/
quality materials + professionalism. This is real voice-of-customer, worth
mining directly for headline copy rather than writing new claims from scratch.

## Primary business/digital problem (one sentence)

Their actual craft and process are strong and well-documented (real testimonials,
organized project photography, a genuine founder story, a real CNC
differentiator) - but almost none of it is working for them online: thin social
reach (116 followers after ~2 years), no clear one-line positioning on the
homepage, and a services page that reads like generic boilerplate instead of
their own much stronger voice. This is a distribution and positioning problem,
not a craft or content problem.
