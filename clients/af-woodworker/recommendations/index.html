What to tag, every time

The Client

Tag the homeowner's own account when they're willing to be tagged. A tagged post shows up on their profile too, reaching the friends and neighbors a direct-to-homeowner shop actually depends on for the next referral.

The Neighborhood

Use Instagram's real location pin on the project's actual neighborhood, Oakland, Mill Valley, Menlo Park, not just the city typed into the caption. This is the lever for showing up in searches from a specific area you want more work in.

The Materials

Tag the hardware, appliance, or material brand when one's actually used, a wine fridge brand, a specific hardware line. A smaller audience, but one already shopping for exactly that kind of project.

Why this matters more than the caption itself: a good caption with no tags reaches your own 116 followers. The same caption with the client, the neighborhood pin, and the material tagged reaches three separate networks, for the same thirty seconds of work.

Get the Reviews

The ask already worked once. Point it at Google next.

Zero reviews on Google and BBB isn't a sign no one's happy, your fifteen Thumbtack reviews prove the opposite. It's a sign the ask has only ever gone out in one place. A short, personal note to your recent clients, the same kind of ask that already produced fifteen reviews once, closes most of this gap directly.

Draft Email, Personalize Before Sending
Subject: A quick favor, from AF Woodworker Hi [Name], It's been a while since we finished [project name or description] together, and we're still proud of how it turned out. We're updating our online presence and realized we've never actually asked, would you be willing to leave us a quick Google review? Even two or three sentences about the project and how it went would mean a lot. [Direct Google review link] Thank you for trusting us with your project. We'd love to work together again. Ahmed AF Woodworker
Why this works: it's personal, not a mass blast, references the real project, and hands over the review link directly so there's no friction between "yes" and the review actually existing.
If there's no reply in a week: one short follow-up, not a second full ask. "Just following up, no pressure either way, but if you have two minutes this week, it'd genuinely help." Most people who leave a review do it on the second nudge, not the first.
