# Image Prompts - AF Woodworker Design Territories

Mood/concept images to help pick a direction, not literal website mockups - AI
image models don't reliably render real layout or legible small UI text, so
each prompt captures the material world, palette, light, and signature visual
device of that territory as a single photographic scene instead. Paste into
nano_banana_pro, Midjourney, or ChatGPT image. Once a territory is picked, the
actual site gets built as HTML per the existing pipeline, not generated as an
image.

---

## Territory A - Precision, Lived In (revised 2026-09-11)

Original version scored as technically interesting but did not make Jennifer
FEEL anything - too cold/spec-sheet. This version leads with a human moment
and demotes the technical annotation to a small, quiet detail.

A warm, natural-light photograph of a hand mid-motion opening a soft-close
kitchen drawer in a real, lived-in kitchen - morning sunlight coming through a
window behind, a coffee cup and a piece of fruit visible on the counter, the
drawer's walnut interior and dovetail joinery visible as it glides open. The
mood is quiet, domestic, and warm - this is someone's real home, not a
showroom. In one small corner of the frame, a single subtle annotation
(a thin line and a small label in a light monospace font reading "CNC-
PROFILED SOFT-CLOSE JOINERY") sits unobtrusively, easy to notice but not
competing with the human moment. Photographic realism, shallow depth of
field, warm golden-hour-adjacent natural light, shot like a lifestyle
photograph, not a product photograph. 16:9.

**What to look for:** does this actually make you feel something before you
notice the technical detail? If the annotation still pulls focus first, the
balance needs to shift further toward the human moment.

## Territory B - Both Of You Should Love It

A clean split-frame product photograph: on the left half, a soft, hand-drawn-
feeling 3D render or sketch of a custom kitchen cabinet design in pale
line-art style on cream paper; on the right half, the same exact cabinet
fully built and installed in a warm, sunlit kitchen, photographed in rich
natural light with warm wood tones and a soft cream/charcoal palette. A thin
vertical divider line separates the two halves, labeled simply "DESIGNED" on
the left and "DELIVERED" on the right in a warm serif typeface. Reassuring,
human, warm mood - soft natural light, no harsh shadows, a sense of a promise
kept. Photographic realism on the right half, soft illustrative rendering on
the left half. 16:9.

**What to look for:** Jennifer's read on this one already worked - the
generated mood came through as intended. The open risk isn't the concept, it's
whether AF Woodworker's ACTUAL renders look this good (see the revision note
in three-territories.md) - this image shows the aspiration, not a guarantee of
their real asset quality.

## Territory C - Two Names, One Shop (revised 2026-09-11)

Original version read too "worn/heritage" - Jennifer doesn't want to imply a
long track record (the company is genuinely young). This version keeps the
real-people, hands-on trust lever but shoots it current and sharp instead of
aged/nostalgic.

A crisp, well-lit editorial photograph of two men in a clean, modern workshop,
mid-conversation over a half-finished wood cabinet piece on a workbench, one
pointing at a tablet showing a 3D design model, bright natural light from a
large window, a modern CNC machine clearly visible and in focus in the
background (not soft-focus). Clean, contemporary color grade - true-to-life
wood tones, crisp whites, sharp modern workwear, no sepia or vintage color
treatment, no dust-in-light or worn-patina styling. Candid, photojournalistic
but energetic, not slow or nostalgic - shot like a feature on a sharp young
company, not a legacy shop. Documentary photography style, bright natural
light, high clarity. 3:2.

**What to look for:** does this read as current and energetic, or does
anything (light quality, styling, mood) still accidentally suggest "long-
established"? The whole point of this territory is trust through direct
access to the makers, not trust through tenure - the image should never imply
decades of history.

## After you look at all three

Note which one(s) pull you in, and whether any element from a "losing"
territory (the spec-card device, the sketch-to-real pairing, the hands-at-work
motif) is worth folding into the winner as a secondary touch rather than a
full territory. The three don't have to be mutually exclusive at the detail
level, even though only one becomes the primary direction.
