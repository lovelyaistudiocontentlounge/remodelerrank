## Territory D - "Precision, Made Modern" (Fabuwood reference)

Derived from Jennifer's reference (fabuwood.com), adapted to what AF
Woodworker actually is: a two-founder bespoke shop, not a manufacturer
selling named product lines through dealers.

Concept name: Precision, Made Modern

Core idea: Fabuwood's confident, geometric, photography-forward register -
which fits AF's own self-description ("modern, sleek, minimalist") far
better than Medallion's ornate register fit SW - carried over directly. Its
two-named-product-line catalog (Allure/Illume) and dealer network do not
carry over: AF builds one-off pieces for direct clients, not a selectable
range through third parties. The "outperform" stats device is kept but
re-grounded entirely in AF's real, honest differentiators (CNC precision,
2D/3D modeling before production, two named founders doing the actual work)
rather than invented performance claims or Fabuwood's outsized "most
desired" scale.

Why it fits: This is the first AF territory built from photography and
positioning confidence rather than a device (spec-card, sketch-pairing,
founder-portrait) that depends on an asset AF may not have ready. It also
sidesteps the earlier feedback problems directly: Territory A's coldness is
fixed by Fabuwood's warmer, styled-but-real photography; Territory C's
age-emphasis risk doesn't apply here since nothing in this territory
references tenure at all.

Typographic direction: A confident modern sans (Fabuwood uses a clean
geometric sans for its wordmark) - General Sans or similar, lowercase
treatment for warmth (mirroring Fabuwood's lowercase "fabuwood" wordmark),
no serif, no script.

Color behavior: Warm mixed woods (real AF photography) against a near-black
or deep charcoal accent surface, matching Fabuwood's black-island-against-
light-wood contrast - a real, replicable device since AF's own workshop
photography already has this warm-wood-on-dark-surface quality.

Layout / composition: Photography-forward hero, large natural-light shots
of finished work, generous negative space around a confident short headline
- not cluttered with the technical annotation Territory A leaned on.

Signature visual device: A "why AF" quantified-differentiators strip -
three or four short, real stat-style claims (their actual CNC/3D-modeling
capability, their actual founder-led process) presented with Fabuwood's
confident brevity, never invented numbers.

Navigation approach: Simple, modern labels (Work / How It's Made / Start a
Project), consistent with the register.

Motion / interaction: Fabuwood itself uses Swiper (a carousel library), not
a GSAP scroll sequence - genuinely simpler to build than the SW hero, a
real advantage here. Photo carousels for project galleries, no scroll-
jacked hero needed.

**Open item, not solved by this territory:** AF Woodworker has no real logo
asset (checked their live Squarespace site directly - plain text only, no
logo image anywhere). Fabuwood's own geometric icon mark is a reasonable
style reference for what a real AF mark could look like eventually (ties to
their engineering/CNC-precision differentiator), but building a fake one
now would be fabricating an asset that doesn't exist. Recommend either
commissioning a real simple mark, or leading with clean wordmark typography
until they have one - Jennifer's call, not something to default past.

What makes this different from the other portfolio sites: unlike Territories
A-C, this one is not built around a single signature device that could fail
if an asset isn't available (spec-cards need technical photography, sketch-
pairing needs their 3D renders, founder-portraits need their buy-in) - it's
built around confidence and photography quality alone, which AF already has
proof of (their existing testimonials and Instagram content).

## Territory A - Product: "Precision, Lived In"

Derived from what they physically make: CNC precision, 2D/3D modeling before
production, wood + acrylic capability.

**Revised 2026-09-11** - original version ("The Spec Sheet") scored as
technically interesting but did not make Jennifer FEEL anything. The fix below
keeps the precision differentiator but demotes it from the dominant visual
grammar to a supporting detail, and puts a real human moment back in the lead.

Concept name: Precision, Lived In

Core idea: Every hero image is a real moment of someone actually living with
the piece - a hand opening a drawer that closes silently, morning light on a
counter, a kid reaching into a pantry - and the technical precision (CNC
detail, joinery, finish) appears only as a small, quiet annotation on that
same photo, not as the photo's whole reason for existing. The feeling comes
first; the craft explains why the feeling is possible.

Why it fits: AF Woodworker's differentiator (CNC + 2D/3D modeling +
engineering background) is real, but leading with it cold reads as a spec
sheet, not a home. Leading with a lived-in human moment and letting precision
explain the feeling afterward keeps the differentiator without losing warmth.

Typographic direction: A warm, confident sans for headlines (General Sans or
similar) with a small monospace accent (IBM Plex Mono) used only for the
quiet spec annotation, not for anything a visitor reads first.

Color behavior: Warm neutral base led by real photography (skin tones, warm
wood, natural light) - the technical accent color (muted blueprint-blue)
appears only inside the small annotation detail, never across a full section.

Layout / composition: Full-bleed lived-in photography leads every section;
the spec annotation is a small, optional-feeling detail in a corner, not a
grid of leader lines across the whole frame.

Photography treatment: Real human moments of use, shot warm and natural - not
clinical product photography. The finished piece is always shown being lived
with, never alone on a white background.

Navigation approach: Simple, warm labels ("The Work," "How It's Made," "Start
a Project") - "How It's Made" is where the precision/CNC story gets its full
due, kept out of the main visual path rather than removed.

Signature visual device: The quiet annotation - one small, human-scale detail
callout per hero photo, easy to notice, never dominant.

Motion / interaction: Restrained - the annotation appears gently on scroll or
hover, never demands attention before the photo itself lands emotionally.

Risks: Needs genuinely good lifestyle/in-use photography, which AF Woodworker
may not currently have (their visible photography is finished-product shots,
not in-use moments) - may require a real photo shoot rather than reusing
existing assets.

What makes this different from the other portfolio sites: leads with feeling
and lets precision earn its place quietly, rather than leading with either
pure spectacle (the old version of this territory) or pure sales copy.

## Territory B - Customer: "Both Of You Should Love It"

Derived from how their ideal customer actually evaluates and buys - their own
testimonials describe an anxious client who needed to feel heard and see the
work evolve safely before committing.

Concept name: Both Of You Should Love It

Core idea: The site is structured as the client's actual de-risking journey -
tell us your vision, see it before we build it, live with it - instead of a
standard service grid. Testimonial language is woven directly into each stage,
not collected in one generic reviews section at the bottom.

Why it fits: Their own reviews reveal the sale is won on trust-in-process, not
price or speed ("asked for my input each step of the way," "would 100% hire
them again"). They already do 2D/3D modeling before production - this
territory turns that into visible reassurance instead of a hidden production
step.

Typographic direction: A warm serif for headlines (Lora or Fraunces) with a
humanist sans for body (Karla or Inter) - softer and more approachable than
Territory A's technical mono pairing.

Color behavior: Warm neutral palette (soft cream, warm charcoal, one wood-tone
accent pulled from real photography) - no cold or technical color anywhere.

Layout / composition: A sequenced journey structure mirroring the actual client
experience, rather than a services grid. Each stage carries its own proof.

Photography treatment: Paired imagery is the core device - a 3D render or
sketch shown directly next to the real finished photo of the SAME piece,
proving the promise-to-delivery match is real and repeatable project after
project.

Navigation approach: Reassurance-first labels instead of jargon - "How it
works," "See real projects," "Start your project" rather than "Services."

Signature visual device: The sketch-to-real "promise match" pairing, used as
the primary portfolio format in place of plain after-photos.

Motion / interaction: A render-to-real crossfade or slider on key projects -
the same before/after mechanism this repo already has
(`scripts/photo-factory/beforeafter.py`), repurposed here for design-to-delivery
rather than a remodel's before/after.

Risks: Depends on AF Woodworker actually having and sharing their 3D renders
per project, not just final photos.

**Revision note 2026-09-11:** this was the concept Jennifer liked best on
first look, but flagged the same dependency risk above as her real concern -
we have not seen an actual AF Woodworker render, only their claim that they
produce them. Two paths forward, not mutually exclusive: (1) ask AF Woodworker
directly for a real render + matching finished photo before committing further
- if their renders look rough or inconsistent, this whole territory is
compromised no matter how good the layout is; (2) a lower-risk fallback that
does not depend on their render quality at all - RemodelerRank creates a
consistent stylized "sketch" treatment FROM the real finished photos ourselves
(a line-art or watercolor-style filter pass, not their internal CAD file),
so the "designed / delivered" pairing stays fully within our own quality
control instead of depending on an asset we have not verified. Recommend (2)
as the default unless (1) turns out to look genuinely great.

What makes this different: Territory A treats their technical process as
spectacle for its own sake; this territory uses the exact same capability (3D
modeling) as emotional proof instead - same underlying fact, opposite use and
opposite visual structure.

## Territory C - Story: "Two Names, One Shop"

Derived from the founders themselves, not from company tenure.

**Revised 2026-09-11** - Jennifer does not want this territory leaning on how
long AF Woodworker has been in business (it's genuinely short, ~3-4 years, and
naming that undercuts trust rather than building it). The fix: keep the real
people, drop every trace of a timeline/experience claim, and root the trust
lever in personal, hands-on involvement instead of years.

Concept name: Two Names, One Shop

Core idea: Ahmed and Fernando are real, named people leading the homepage from
the first frame - not because they've been doing this for decades, but because
every piece is still made and touched by the two people whose names are on the
door. The trust lever is direct personal involvement ("you work with the
people who actually build it, not an account manager"), never duration.

Why it fits: The engineer-plus-craftsman partnership is their most distinctive
and hardest-to-copy asset regardless of age - a bigger, anonymous competitor
cannot offer direct access to its actual makers. This works precisely because
it does not depend on tenure.

Typographic direction: A confident, contemporary sans (Neue Haas or General
Sans) for a magazine-profile feel, paired with a plain, honest body sans -
different register from both A (warm/lived-in) and B (soft/reassuring). Should
read current and energetic, not heritage or legacy.

Color behavior: Photography-led and muted - background stays neutral so real
portrait and workshop photography carries the color, with one accent pulled
from an actual material or tool seen in real photos, not an abstract brand
color. Avoid any patina/aged/vintage color treatment - this should feel
sharp and current, not like a legacy shop.

Layout / composition: Editorial/profile structure - opens on Ahmed and
Fernando in the real shop, working, not posed - then moves into the work as
"the two people who build every piece here," with no timeline or "since
[year]" framing anywhere in the copy.

Photography treatment: Documentary/behind-the-scenes leads - hands, tools, the
CNC machine running - intercut with finished-piece photography, so process and
product carry equal weight. Photography should feel crisp and current (clean
light, sharp focus, modern workshop), not aged or nostalgic - avoid anything
that visually reads as "long-established."

Navigation approach: A narrative scroll on the homepage for people who want the
story, with a simple secondary nav for people who just want to see the work.

Signature visual device: A recurring "hands at work" motif - detail shots of
hands, tools, or the machine at each section transition, reinforcing that
named people, not an anonymous crew, make every piece.

Motion / interaction: Understated parallax on portrait/workshop photography
only - restraint everywhere else, consistent with the other two territories.

Risks: Needs Ahmed and Fernando's real buy-in to be the public face - if they
are camera-shy or prefer staying behind the work, this territory does not
work. Also needs discipline in copywriting: any stray "since 2022" or "X years
of experience" line undoes the whole point - trust here comes from access to
the makers, not from track record.

What makes this different: Territory A is about the product/feeling,
Territory B is about the client's experience, this one is about the makers as
people - three different heroes built from the same underlying business, none
of them dependent on this being an old company.

## Anti-cloning check (before presenting)

Strip logo, colors, and photos from all three: they remain distinguishable by
information architecture alone - A is annotated spec-cards on a drafting grid,
B is a sequenced client-journey with sketch-to-real pairing as the core
portfolio format, C is an editorial profile-scroll leading with named people.
None of the three signature devices (spec-card overlay / sketch-to-real pairing
/ hands-at-work motif) would transplant cleanly onto a different cabinet
company without that company sharing the same underlying fact (real CNC
process / real 3D renders per project / real willing founders) - each is
derived from something specific to AF Woodworker, not a reusable trick.
