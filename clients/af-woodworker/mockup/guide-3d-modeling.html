Design Process

Why We Model Every Piece in 3D Before We Build It

Short answer Full 2D and 3D modeling before production lets you see the finished cabinetry, exact dimensions, finishes, and how it fits your actual space, before a single cut is made. It catches sizing and layout problems on screen instead of mid-build, where they cost real time and material to fix.

Most cabinet shops work from a sketch and a materials list. AF Woodworker starts every project, kitchen, closet, built-in, or a CNC fabrication piece, as a full 2D and 3D model of the real space. That's not a rendering for marketing, it's the actual plan the cabinetry gets built from.

What this step actually prevents

A cabinet run that's off by even a quarter inch against a real wall, appliance gap, or window trim doesn't show up until install day if it was never modeled against the real space. Modeling first means Ahmed and Fernando are working against the actual room's dimensions from the start, not a generic template scaled to roughly fit.

It also means you're approving what the piece will actually look like, finish, hardware, proportions, before anything is committed to material. Changing a design on screen costs nothing. Changing it after the cabinets are built costs real time and real material.

How it fits into a real project

The model comes first, then fabrication happens in-house, including any CNC detail work the piece calls for, then installation is handled by the same two people who designed it. Nothing gets handed off between a designer, a builder, and an installer who've never talked to each other.
