What to Know Before Hiring a Remodeling Contractor in the East Bay

Hiring the wrong contractor is one of the most expensive mistakes a homeowner can make. These are the questions that matter - answered straight, without the sales spin.

What should I look for when hiring a general contractor in the East Bay?

The most important factors are licensure, insurance, and a track record of finishing on schedule. In California, every general contractor must hold an active CSLB license - you can verify any contractor's license at cslb.ca.gov before signing anything.

Beyond the license, look for a contractor who gives you a written scope of work, a fixed price (not an estimate that grows), and a realistic timeline they are willing to put in writing. A good contractor will not be offended by those requests. A bad one will stall or change the subject.

Contractors who work consistently in Contra Costa and Alameda counties - places like Walnut Creek, Danville, and San Ramon - will know the local permit offices, the inspection timelines, and the subcontractors who actually show up. That local knowledge matters more than a low bid from someone who drives in from two counties away.

How much does a kitchen remodel cost in Walnut Creek or Danville?

In the East Bay, a mid-range kitchen remodel typically runs between $60,000 and $120,000 depending on size, materials, and whether the layout changes. A full high-end remodel with custom cabinetry, stone countertops, and appliance upgrades can reach $150,000 or more.

The biggest drivers of cost are:

  • Layout changes - moving plumbing or gas lines adds $5,000-$15,000
  • Cabinet quality - stock vs. semi-custom vs. full custom is the biggest single variable
  • Countertop material - quartz vs. marble vs. quartzite shifts the number by $3,000-$12,000
  • Permits - Contra Costa County requires permits for most kitchen remodels. Budget $1,500-$3,000 and 3-6 weeks for approval
Be skeptical of bids under $40,000 for a full kitchen remodel in this market. They usually mean unlicensed labor, skipped permits, or a scope that quietly excludes half the work.
How long does a kitchen or bathroom remodel take in the East Bay?

A kitchen remodel with a permit typically takes 8 to 14 weeks from permit approval to final inspection. A bathroom remodel runs 4 to 8 weeks. These timelines assume materials are ordered before demolition starts - a contractor who orders cabinets after demo begins is the reason projects drag on for months.

The biggest time variable in Contra Costa County is the permit process. Walnut Creek and Danville can take 4-8 weeks for approval; Concord and Antioch are often faster. A contractor who pulls permits regularly in your city will know how to front-load the process so inspections do not become a bottleneck mid-project.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel or room addition in Contra Costa County?

Room additions always require a permit. There are no exceptions in Contra Costa County.

For bathroom remodels, it depends on the scope:

  • Replacing fixtures in the same location - usually no permit required
  • Moving plumbing, adding electrical circuits, or changing the layout - permit required
  • Converting a half-bath to a full bath - permit required

A licensed general contractor will tell you upfront which permits your project needs and will pull them on your behalf. If a contractor tells you permits are not required for work that clearly needs them, that is a red flag - unpermitted work can affect your homeowner's insurance and your ability to sell the home.

How do I verify a contractor's license in California?

Go to cslb.ca.gov and search by license number or business name. You want to confirm three things:

  • The license is active and in good standing - not expired, suspended, or revoked
  • The license classification matches the work - a "B" general contractor license covers most remodeling; specialty work may require specific classifications
  • Workers' compensation insurance is on file - this protects you if a worker is injured on your property

Do this before any money changes hands. The CSLB lookup takes about 30 seconds and it has saved more homeowners from costly mistakes than any other step in the hiring process.

What questions should I ask a contractor before signing anything?

These six questions separate the professionals from the rest:

  • Can I see your CSLB license number and proof of insurance? - any legitimate contractor will share these without hesitation
  • Will you pull all required permits? - the answer should always be yes
  • Who are your subcontractors and how long have you worked with them? - a contractor with a stable trade network is far less likely to have scheduling gaps
  • Can you give me a written schedule with milestone dates? - if they cannot commit to a schedule before signing, they will not commit to one after
  • What does your payment schedule look like? - never pay more than 10% or $1,000 upfront (whichever is less) in California; the CSLB sets this limit by law
  • What happens if we find unexpected issues behind the walls? - how they answer this tells you everything about how they handle surprises
What is an ADU and is it worth building in the East Bay?

An ADU - accessory dwelling unit - is a permitted secondary living space on a single-family property. It can be attached to the main home, detached in the backyard, or converted from an existing garage or basement.

In the East Bay, ADUs are almost always worth building for homeowners with the right lot. Rental rates in Walnut Creek, San Ramon, and Danville run $1,800 to $2,800 per month for a one-bedroom unit. A well-built ADU in this market typically costs $150,000-$250,000 all-in and generates enough income to cover most or all of the construction financing.

California law now requires most cities to approve ADU applications within 60 days. Contra Costa County has been consistent about this timeline. A contractor who has built ADUs in your city will know what the planning department looks for and can design to approval rather than designing and then revising.
