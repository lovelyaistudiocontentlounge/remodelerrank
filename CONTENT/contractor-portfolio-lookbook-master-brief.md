# MASTER BUILD BRIEF
## Contractor-Focused Digital Portfolio + Interactive Sales Experience + Physical Lookbook

### PROJECT PURPOSE

Build a portfolio and sales system for a marketing/business systems consultant who primarily works with construction, cabinetry, remodeling, home-service, manufacturing, and trades businesses.

This should NOT feel like a traditional marketing agency website.

Avoid:
- generic agency layouts
- endless website mockups
- overly editorial experiences
- artsy navigation
- complicated interactions
- excessive copy
- trendy animations with no purpose
- generic "elevate your brand" language
- identical client websites with swapped logos/colors/photos
- overly masculine construction cliches such as blueprint backgrounds, sawblades, diamond plate, wood textures, sparks, hard hats, etc.

The audience is primarily busy male business owners in construction and related trades.

They should understand the point almost immediately.

The desired reaction is:

"That's clever."

followed quickly by:

"Shit. I wonder what's wrong with mine."

The overall philosophy:

**Sophisticated idea. Simple execution.**

Borrow the clarity and restraint of premium automotive advertising, Scandinavian product advertising, architectural product catalogs, premium building-material lookbooks, and high-end construction photography.

Do NOT make the interface behave like an experimental art portfolio.

---

# CORE BRAND IDEA

The central analogy is:

## IT CAN LOOK FINISHED AND STILL NOT BE BUILT RIGHT.

A contractor can walk into a completed kitchen and immediately notice problems or missed opportunities that the average homeowner will never see.

The homeowner sees:

"Beautiful kitchen."

The experienced contractor/cabinetmaker/designer sees:

- poor alignment
- bad clearances
- awkward proportions
- inexpensive finishing details
- missed opportunities
- functional conflicts
- installation mistakes
- details that separate acceptable work from excellent work

Digital marketing works the same way.

A business owner sees:

"We have a website."

"We post on Instagram."

"We have a Google profile."

"We answer leads."

Nothing necessarily looks catastrophically broken.

A digital strategist sees:

- weak geographic signals
- generic copy
- unclear service areas
- poor project descriptions
- weak calls to action
- missing search context
- poor information hierarchy
- inadequate lead capture
- slow or nonexistent follow-up
- weak review strategy
- beautiful social content without useful context
- poor internal linking
- missing structured information
- inconsistent positioning
- lost conversion opportunities

The portfolio should demonstrate this idea rather than merely explain it.

# EXPERIENCE 01
# "WHAT'S WRONG WITH THIS PICTURE?"

This is the opening campaign concept.

Inspired by old children's "What's Wrong With This Picture?" / "Find the Mistakes" puzzles, but executed with sophisticated, photorealistic, high-end architectural imagery.

Do NOT make it childish.

## Hero

Display a gorgeous, highly photorealistic Northern California luxury kitchen.

Visual character:
- sophisticated custom cabinetry
- expensive but believable
- warm modern / transitional architecture
- excellent natural light
- high-quality stone
- premium appliances
- restrained styling
- looks like a $100K+ kitchen
- believable construction details

Headline:

**YOU KNOW WHAT'S WRONG WITH THIS KITCHEN.**

Supporting text:

**Can you find all 5?**

The image contains exactly five deliberately designed problems.

Potential examples:

1. DRAWER / APPLIANCE COLLISION
A drawer cannot properly operate because of an adjacent oven/appliance clearance conflict.

2. SCONCE ALIGNMENT
Two architectural sconces around a window are subtly misaligned or inconsistently spaced.

3. OUTLET TREATMENT
Cheap bright-white plastic outlet cover sitting conspicuously against premium stone.

4. MILLWORK / CROWN / TRIM DETAIL
An awkward termination, inconsistent reveal, alignment issue, or other realistic finish-carpentry problem.

5. CABINET PROPORTION / ALIGNMENT DETAIL
Something an experienced cabinetmaker would notice but an average homeowner might overlook.

IMPORTANT:
Every construction mistake must be physically plausible.

Do not invent fake building-code violations.

Do not make the kitchen absurd or obviously broken.

The entire point is that the image initially looks excellent.

# PUZZLE INTERACTION

Desktop:
Allow users to inspect the image.

Potential interaction:
- click/tap suspected problem
- subtle feedback
- optional progress: "2 of 5 found"

Mobile:
Do not require precision clicking.

Provide an easy:
**SHOW ME**

option.

Never trap a user in the game.

The portfolio must remain usable for someone who has zero interest in playing.

After interaction OR scrolling, reveal the five issues with restrained numbered annotations.

Avoid cartoon circles.

Use architectural-style:
- fine leader lines
- small numbers
- subtle markers
- clean typography

Reveal copy:

## YOU SAW THINGS MOST PEOPLE WOULDN'T.

Body:

That's what happens when you've spent years learning your trade.

You know the difference between finished and finished right.

Then:

## I DO THE SAME THING HERE.

Transition visually from kitchen/build environment into digital business environment.

# SIGNATURE VISUAL CONCEPT

Every client website gets ONE primary visual idea.

Not ten tricks.

The idea must:

- communicate something specific about that company
- derive from product/process/history/customer/differentiator
- be understandable quickly
- work on mobile
- avoid interfering with navigation
- create a memorable hero/campaign moment

Example principle:

A finished interior photograph transitions into an isolated view of only the cabinetry the company produced.

This demonstrates what the company contributed to the space.

Other companies must NOT automatically reuse this treatment.

Each client requires its own signature visual concept.

# ANTI-CLONING TEST

Before approving any design:

1. Remove the logo.
2. Remove the company name.
3. Replace photography with neutral gray blocks.
4. Compare it against every other portfolio website.

Ask:

**Can I still tell these websites were art-directed for different businesses?**

If NO:

REJECT OR REWORK.

Changing:
- logo
- colors
- photos

does NOT constitute unique art direction.

Meaningful differentiation should appear in:
- typography
- proportions
- information hierarchy
- composition
- navigation
- image behavior
- spacing
- storytelling
- interaction
- visual rhythm
- signature device

---

# CLIENT WEBSITE UX RULE

The client's site should remain easy for a busy person to use.

Prioritize:

- immediate understanding
- obvious navigation
- visible contact path
- strong mobile experience
- quick project browsing
- fast loading
- accessibility
- readable typography
- restrained motion

The hero may contain an art-directed "wow."

The rest of the site should be extremely usable.

Goal:

**One creative idea executed with restraint.**

- large imagery
- minimal copy
- before/after scrubbers

- touch-friendly controls

Do not require internet-dependent interactions for the core presentation if an offline implementation is practical.

---

# RESPONSIVE PRIORITY

Design in this order:

1. MOBILE
2. TABLET
3. DESKTOP
4. PRINT

Do not design desktop first and simply stack everything vertically for mobile.

The construction audience is highly likely to encounter the work from a phone.

Every signature interaction must have a simple mobile equivalent.

---

# PERFORMANCE

Avoid a portfolio that takes forever to load because it contains enormous project imagery.

Implement:
- responsive image sizes
- modern image formats
- lazy loading
- optimized video
- poster frames
- sensible animation libraries
- reduced-motion support
- image compression

Performance is part of the demonstration of competence.
